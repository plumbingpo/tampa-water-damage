Typical Water Damage Problems in Sarasota

Not every water problem announces itself with a flooded floor. Below are the situations our crews are called to most often around Sarasota, and what each one tends to involve.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Clues include flooring that feels spongy, blistered paint and a higher water bill you cannot explain. Thermal imaging and moisture readings let us map how far it spread before anything gets cut open. Even a slow leak matters, because wood and drywall that stay damp will rot and grow mold.

Sewage: A blocked sewer line sends waste back into the house, usually at the lowest shower, toilet or floor drain. Tree roots, grease buildup and heavy rain overloading the public system are the common triggers. This is Category 3 water, which carries bacteria and should not be handled without protective gear. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. From there it comes in under doors, through window wells and across garage floors. Because surface water carries dirt and chemicals from the ground it passed over, it is handled as unsanitary. A big enough storm can cause it at any time of year, and Sarasota properties are no exception.

Crawlspace: The space beneath a raised floor is easy to forget, which lets dampness go unchecked for years. Humidity stays high down there when a pipe drips, the ground cover is damaged or water pools after rain. Over time that leads to sagging insulation, musty odors indoors and soft or cupped flooring. Our team removes the water, dries the framing and can point out what is letting moisture in.

Condensate: An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid day. That water should leave through a small drain line, which clogs easily with algae and dust. When it backs up, the pan overflows into the ceiling, closet or hallway around the air handler. Most owners learn of it from a ceiling stain or drooping drywall, so have the drain line cleared once a year.
