Bathroom Water Damage Help in Tampa

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. In Tampa, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

What It Looks Like

  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub
  • Vinyl flooring that curls at the edges or has discolored near the toilet or tub
  • The bottom panel inside the vanity has swelled, stained or bowed
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little
  • A hollow sound, movement, or loose tiles in the floor by the bathtub
Our Process

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.

Leaking Shower Pan

With a crack in the liner or pan, water escapes below the shower floor at each use. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

Trapped Steam

Without a working exhaust fan, the steam from showers has nowhere to go. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

The Room Below

Water follows pipes and joists before it drips, so the stain downstairs may sit several feet from the actual leak. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Gets Past

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.
