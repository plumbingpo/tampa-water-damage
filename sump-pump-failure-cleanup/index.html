Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in Tampa

The moment a basement most needs its sump pump is often the moment the pump gives out. Groundwater rises in a big storm and pours into the pit, right as the electricity fails, the float jams or the inflow outruns the pump. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

Do not think of it as clean water. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Keep Out of the BasementDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Water may be touching outlets, the furnace or the pump's own cord.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceSee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Get the Water Out

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Carpet, padding, insulation and wet drywall that the water contacted are pulled by a crew in protective gear, bagged and removed from the property so the concrete and studs are open.

3

Wash and Disinfect

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Confirm

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Losing electricity in a storm when the pump has no battery backup.
  • Outside the house, a crushed, clogged or separated discharge pipe.
  • A failed check valve that lets pumped water run back into the pit.
  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
  • A pump too small for the volume of water entering the pit.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Carpet and Flooring

Groundwater-soaked carpet and padding come out, as cleaning cannot make them safe. Laminate and vinyl plank trap silty water underneath and usually come up too.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

Mechanical equipment often shares the floor with the sump pit. Controls, wiring and burners can be destroyed by water, so every appliance needs an inspection before it runs again.

Outlets and Wiring

Basement outlets sit low on the wall, where rising water can reach them. Before those circuits are used again, an electrician needs to inspect whatever was under water.

Boxes and Stored Items

Books, cloth, boxes and wood furniture legs take in dirty water within a short time. Anything porous that sat in it is hard to disinfect and will grow mold within days.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. To keep the claim file complete, our team documents the damage, the failed pump and the height of the water.
