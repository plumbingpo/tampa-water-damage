Attic Water Damage in Tampa

Attic water damage comes from two directions. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. In Tampa, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Heating and cooling bills go up because cellulose and fiberglass alike give up R-value when wet. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

Warning Signs

  • Light from outside visible along a roof valley or around a chimney or vent pipe
  • A musty smell in the attic or in the upstairs closets
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • Attic ducts and metal hangers showing drips, rust or water marks
  • Sheathing with fuzzy growth, black or white, concentrated toward the eaves
  • A bath fan or ceiling light fixture with drips or a stain around it

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Can Start

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. The drywall may sag, and rings of brown staining appear on the ceiling of the room below.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Ceiling Collapse

Insulation full of water weighs a lot. Wet from above, drywall loses strength at its fasteners and may fall without warning, with the wet insulation coming down too.

Lost Insulation Value

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

Some bathroom fans exhaust into the attic and never reach the outdoors, adding warm, damp air daily. That moisture condenses on the cold sheathing and looks just like a roof leak.

Leaks From Attic Equipment

In a lot of homes the attic houses an air handler along with its condensate line and pan. For weeks an overflowing pan or blocked drain can drip into insulation, and there is no storm to tip you off.

Our Process

How We Handle Attic Water Damage

1

Inspect and Find the Source

We check the roof deck, flashing points, vents and exhaust ducts to learn whether the water is a leak, condensation, or both. Its full reach is mapped with a thermal camera and meter readings.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

Insulation that is soaked or has mold gets bagged and carried out, giving the wood and the drywall below a chance to dry. We protect the living space below and keep the access path contained.

3

Dry the Framing

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. An antimicrobial goes on after any visible mold has been cleaned from the wood.

4

Fix the Cause

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. Once the attic stays dry, new insulation goes in to restore the R-value.
