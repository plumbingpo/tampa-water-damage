Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Tampa

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our Tampa crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceElectric units get shut down at the breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Rust at the connections above the tank, either the flex lines or the inlet and outlet fittings.
  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
  • Years of sediment buildup and corrosion wearing a hole in the base of an old steel tank.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Stored Belongings

Boxes, furniture and keepsakes stored near a garage or closet heater sit in the water first. Cardboard falls apart, and wet contents can mold if they are not dried or discarded quickly.

Closet Walls

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. Swollen baseboards and a wall bottom that is still damp within are typical.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.

Attic and Ceilings

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Wet insulation stops insulating and its weight can bring ceiling drywall down.

Coverage

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • If water got to the flooring of adjoining rooms, and the kind of flooring involved.
  • The volume of wet stored items that must be dried, moved or disposed of.
  • The location of the heater, since a garage leak costs less than one in an interior closet or attic.
  • How long the tank kept refilling before the supply valve was closed.

Our Process

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Extract the Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Inspect the Surrounding Area

We go over the wall bases, the platform, any ceiling underneath and the adjoining rooms with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which shows how far the wet area extends.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

We take out baseboards that are damaged, drywall that is crumbling and insulation that is soaked. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Dry and Monitor

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for the 3 to 5 days drying usually takes. We record moisture readings and tell you when the space is ready for the new heater.
